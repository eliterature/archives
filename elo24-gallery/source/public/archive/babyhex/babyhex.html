Notes

BabyHex is a fictional virtual child, who was inspired by another actual virtual child, who is based on an actual biological child. I know. It confuses us too.

“BabyX” was developed by Mark Sagar and his team at Soul Machines in 2013, and created in the likeness of his own daughter when she was around two years old. For the narrative, I’ve drawn on some of the details in a 2017 interview by Ashlee Vance, “Mark Sagar Made a Baby in His Lab: Now It Plays the Piano” (Businessweek). One of the books visible in the cover photo for that article is by Paul Bloom, who I paraphrase in segment three on the upshot of perversity. That’s from his 2023 TED Talk on “The surprising psychology behind your urge to break the rules.”

For fictionalizing some of the technical workings of BabyHex’s system, I used this article published by Martin Takac, Alistair Knott, and Mark Sagar in 2020: “C-block: A system for learning motor plans with perceptual consequences.”
The philosopher-historian I reference in the opening is Jean-Pierre Dupuy. In his 2000 book, The Mechanization of the Mind, Dupuy writes about the origins of cybernetics at the so-called Macy Conferences. Here’s the full passage, which always stuck with me as identifying a formative moment in the intellectual history of simulation:
Reality had become a mere means to higher end: model making. […] But more often, and then usually in an unreflective way, object and model exhibited an awkward tendency during these discussions to constantly switch roles. The ambivalence of the very word model […] was thus revealed: the model may be either that which imitates or that which is, or deserves to be, imitated.
In segment IV, I cite a line from Siobhan O’Flynn’s wonderful "Ten Maxims for Guiding Shy, Reluctant, or Unruly AI” (2022).

Other aesthetic inputs include Erik Loyer’s Lair of the Marrow Monkey (1998) and Chroma (2001) and in particular his wildly inventive “mnemonic membrane.”

And of course, there is the me[z]merizing allure of [Por]TrAIts (2022), and the MALFI Trials (1996).
